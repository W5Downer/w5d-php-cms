## 2. Dossier: W5D Macro Works

The five macro works are the **top-level demand map** of the W5D ontology. Each macro contains 26 registered services, a common 1,000-expert capacity convention and a distinct placement in the Real/Virtual spectrum.

### 2.1 Cultures / Humanities — services #001–#026

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Placement** | Real 1.0 |
| **Domain question** | How do societies remember, interpret, translate and transmit meaning? |
| **Representative services** | Heritage Digitisation (#001), Language Preservation (#002), Cultural Tourism Design (#003), Museum Experience Engineering (#004), Oral History (#005), Translation & Localisation Hubs (#006), Digital Humanities Labs (#007), Arts & Creative Economy (#008), Publishing & WebBooks (#009), Film & Audiovisual Heritage (#010), Manuscript Studies (#020), Heritage Conservation Science (#025), Cultural Data Governance (#026) |
| **Typical expert fields** | Archaeology, linguistics, museum studies, translation, film preservation, conservation, cultural economics |
| **Characteristic output unit** | Accepted capture packages, annotated recordings, translated words, restored footage minutes, catalogue records |
| **Recurring boundary** | Custodial permission and community consent precede publication; Dyon never substitutes for statutory heritage authority |

The macro is explicitly **not an IT-only demand map**: heritage conservation combines materials science, building safety and digital access; translation hubs feed science communication and digital governance. Its services feed Built Environment (heritage-built), Sciences (research-data, official-stats) and Governance (municipal-capacity).

### 2.2 Built Environment — services #027–#052

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Placement** | Real 1.0 |
| **Domain question** | How are shelter, infrastructure and inhabited space designed, delivered and maintained? |
| **Representative services** | Affordable Housing Delivery (#027), Urban Planning & Zoning (#028), Infrastructure Rehabilitation (#029), Smart Buildings & IoT (#030), Water & Sanitation Infrastructure (#035), Energy-Efficient Retrofits (#036), Transport & Mobility Planning (#037), Disaster-Resilient Construction (#038), BIM (#040), Public Space Design (#041), Coastal & Flood Defence (#048), Participatory Neighbourhood Upgrading (#052) |
| **Typical expert fields** | Civil engineering, architecture, urban planning, surveying, building services, hydraulics |
| **Characteristic output unit** | Design package reviews, asset inspections, leak survey kilometres, condition surveys, zoning layers |
| **Recurring boundary** | Affordability includes operating costs, not sale price alone; draft zoning remains advisory until competent authority validation |

This macro carries the **heaviest physical-infrastructure load** and the most explicit dependence on the November 2023 CCDR findings (SONEDE losses of 34% in 2021). It feeds Sciences (EIA, climate modelling), Governance (municipal capacity, utility regulation) and Technology (BIM, construction-tech deployment, renewable systems).

### 2.3 Sciences — services #053–#078

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Placement** | Between 1.0 |
| **Domain question** | How is reliable knowledge produced, measured, validated and translated into decision-grade evidence? |
| **Representative services** | Clinical Research Coordination (#053), Public Health Surveillance (#054), Water & Soil Quality Labs (#055), Agricultural Science & Extension (#056), Marine & Coastal Science (#057), Climate Modelling & Adaptation (#058), Materials Science & Testing (#059), Bioinformatics & Genomics (#060), Metrology & Standards (#064), Chemical Safety & Toxicology (#065), Biodiversity & Conservation Science (#067), Renewable Energy Science (#068), Food Safety & Nutrition Science (#069), Social Science Research Methods (#073), Laboratory Accreditation Support (#075), STEM Education & Teacher Training (#076), Environmental Impact Assessment (#078) |
| **Typical expert fields** | Epidemiology, analytical chemistry, agronomy, marine biology, materials engineering, metrology, biostatistics |
| **Characteristic output unit** | Accessioned samples, validated assay results, pipeline validation runs, calibration certificates, species observation records |
| **Recurring boundary** | Research analyses cannot become clinical diagnoses without authorised validation; laboratories publish method limits and chain of custody |

The “Between 1.0” placement reflects the macro’s **laboratory-and-field hybrid character**: it is not purely Real (many outputs are data and method) and not purely Virtual (physical samples, field stations and accredited facilities are load-bearing). Its services feed every other macro, but most critically Governance (health, climate, utility regulation) and Technology (biotech, medical devices, renewable systems).

### 2.4 Governance — services #079–#104

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Placement** | Between 1.0 |
| **Domain question** | How are rules made, legitimate decisions taken, public money allocated and accountability maintained? |
| **Representative services** | Regulatory Impact Assessment (#079), Legal Drafting & Legislative Review (#080), Anti-Corruption & Integrity (#081), Public Procurement Reform (#082), Digital Governance & e-Services (#083), Data Protection & Privacy (#084), Municipal Capacity Building (#085), Rule of Law & Access to Justice (#086), Tax Administration Modernisation (#087), Social Protection Delivery (#088), Climate Governance & NDCs (#090), Public Financial Management (#091), Elections & Civic Participation (#092), Platform & AI Governance (#093), Labour Market Governance (#094), Land Governance & Cadastre (#095), Health Governance (#097), Education Governance (#098), Statistics & Official Data (#100), Crisis & Emergency Governance (#101), State-Owned Enterprise Oversight (#102), Decentralisation & Local Finance (#103), Treaty & International Cooperation (#104) |
| **Typical expert fields** | Law, public administration, economics, audit, social policy, statistics, international relations |
| **Characteristic output unit** | Regulatory option briefs, draft clause packages, integrity risk reviews, cash forecast reviews, MRV dataset reviews |
| **Recurring boundary** | Only authorised institutions adopt law, set tariffs, declare emergencies or award accreditation; W5D supports evidence and referrals, not statutory powers |

This is the **accountability macro**. Its “Between 1.0” placement signals that governance work is neither purely documentary nor purely physical: it requires legitimate authority, administrative records and implementation owners. It is the macro most dependent on **named beneficiaries** — an advisory product without a public-service partner cannot be assumed to improve an institution.

### 2.5 Technology — services #105–#130

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Placement** | Real 0.5 / Virtual 0.5 (register: Virtual core for #105–#117; Real instantiation for #118–#130) |
| **Domain question** | How are deployable technical capabilities built, secured, integrated and maintained? |
| **Representative services** | Software Engineering Teams (#105), Cybersecurity Operations (#106), Cloud & Infrastructure (#107), Data Engineering & Analytics (#108), AI & Machine Learning (#109), Network Engineering (#110), Digital Identity & Authentication (#111), Platform Engineering (#112), Geospatial & Remote Sensing (#113), Quantum & Advanced Computing (#114), Blockchain & Distributed Systems (#115), Robotics Software (#116), IT Governance & Compliance (#117), Construction Technology Deployment (#118), Renewable Energy Systems (#119), Water Treatment Technology (#120), Biotech & Lab Automation (#121), Advanced Manufacturing (#122), Agricultural Technology (#123), Energy Efficiency Audits (#124), Waste-to-Resource Technology (#125), Medical Device & Diagnostics Tech (#126), Mining & Materials Processing Tech (#127), Transportation Technology (#128), Cold Chain & Logistics Tech (#129), Disaster Response Technology (#130) |
| **Typical expert fields** | Computer science, engineering, applied mathematics, cybersecurity, robotics, biomedical engineering |
| **Characteristic output unit** | Reviewed feature increments, alert investigations, validated pipeline changes, infrastructure change reviews, site device commissioning tests |
| **Recurring boundary** | Operators approve changes; model deployment requires a defined use case, baseline comparison and human escalation; testing requires written scope |

The macro is internally split: **#105–#117 are “Virtual core”** (primarily digital deliverables), while **#118–#130 are “Real instantiation”** (technology deployed into physical systems — construction sites, water plants, farms, clinics, mines, cold chains). The “Real 0.5 / Virtual 0.5” label is an **organisational convention**, not a measured fraction of hours.

### 2.6 Cross-macro structure at a glance

| Macro | Services | Placement | Primary output character | Most frequent downstream consumers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cultures / Humanities | 26 | Real 1.0 | Curated, rights-cleared cultural artifacts | Built Environment, Sciences, Governance |
| Built Environment | 26 | Real 1.0 | Physical design, inspection and rehabilitation packages | Sciences, Governance, Technology |
| Sciences | 26 | Between 1.0 | Validated measurements, methods and evidence | Governance, Technology, Built Environment |
| Governance | 26 | Between 1.0 | Legitimate decisions, rules, financial and accountability instruments | All macros via authorising environment |
| Technology | 26 | Real 0.5 / Virtual 0.5 | Digital and deployed technical systems | All macros via tooling and infrastructure |

The five-macro set is an **ontology of need**, not a sales menu. Its coherence comes from the fact that every service declares at least one other-macro input provider and one subsequent-week consumer, forcing cross-domain coordination rather than siloed delivery.
